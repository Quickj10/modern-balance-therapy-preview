Your health information and your rights

Notice of privacy practices.

Last updated November 18, 2026

In short

  • Your information is used for your treatment, for payment, and to run the practice. Almost everything else needs your written permission.
  • Psychotherapy notes get extra protection and are never shared without a specific authorization, with narrow legal exceptions.
  • You can see your record, ask for corrections, ask how you are contacted, and complain to the practice or to the federal government without any penalty.

This notice describes how medical information about you may be used and disclosed and how you can get access to this information. Please review it carefully.

Effective date: [Effective date, usually the launch date]. This notice applies to Modern Balance Therapy and its sole provider, Jackie McGreggor, MA, LPC, a Licensed Professional Counselor in Texas (the "practice").

The practice's duties

The practice is required by law to keep your protected health information (PHI) private, to give you this notice of its legal duties and privacy practices, to follow the terms of the notice currently in effect, and to tell you if a breach of your unsecured PHI occurs.

How the practice may use and disclose your information without your authorization

Treatment

To provide and coordinate your care. For example, with your knowledge, consulting another provider involved in your care, or coordinating with your physician when you have asked for that.

Payment

To bill and collect payment. Modern Balance Therapy is a private-pay practice and does not bill insurance. If you ask for a superbill to submit to your own plan, it will include the information a plan requires, such as dates of service, service codes and a diagnosis code, and you decide whether to submit it.

Health care operations

To run the practice responsibly: quality review, clinical consultation and supervision, training, record keeping, compliance, and business planning. Business associates who perform services for the practice, such as the practice management and video platform, sign agreements requiring them to protect your information.

Uses and disclosures that require your written authorization

  • Psychotherapy notes. Notes kept separately that record the content of your sessions get extra protection. They are not shared without your specific written authorization, except for the limited situations the law allows (for example, the practice's own treatment use, supervision, defense in a legal action you bring, or as required by law).
  • Marketing. Your information is never used for marketing without your authorization.
  • Sale of information. Your information is never sold.
  • Anything else not described in this notice requires your written authorization. You may revoke an authorization in writing at any time, except to the extent the practice has already acted on it.

Disclosures the law may require or permit without your authorization

  • As required by law. When federal, state or local law requires it.
  • Abuse, neglect or exploitation. Texas law requires licensed counselors to report suspected abuse or neglect of a child, and suspected abuse, neglect or exploitation of an elderly person or an adult with a disability.
  • Serious threat to health or safety. If you present a serious and imminent threat of harm to yourself or another person, the practice may disclose what is necessary to prevent the harm, which in Texas may include notifying medical or law enforcement personnel.
  • Health oversight. To the Texas Behavioral Health Executive Council or other oversight agencies for audits, investigations and licensing activities.
  • Judicial and administrative proceedings. In response to a court order, or in some situations a subpoena, after the protections the law gives you have been satisfied. The practice will tell you about a request for your records where the law allows.
  • Public health, workers' compensation, coroners and specialized government functions, in the narrow situations defined by law.
  • To people involved in your care, such as a family member you have identified, when you agree or, if you are unable to agree, when the practice reasonably believes it is in your best interest and limited to what that person needs to know.

Where Texas law is more protective of your privacy than federal law, the practice follows Texas law.

Clients ages 12 to 17

A parent or guardian consents to treatment for a minor and has rights of access to records under Texas law, with limited exceptions. The practice explains to both the teen and the parent what stays private and what will be shared before treatment begins, and keeps that agreement consistent.

Telehealth

Sessions are held on a secure, HIPAA-compliant video platform covered by a Business Associate Agreement. Please do not send clinical details by regular email, text message or through the website forms; they are not secure and are not part of your record. Use the client portal or a phone call. The practice will confirm your location at the start of each session, as Texas licensing rules require you to be in Texas during care.

Your rights

  • Access. You may see or get a copy of your record, in paper or electronic form, with limited exceptions such as psychotherapy notes. The practice may charge a reasonable, cost-based fee for copies.
  • Amendment. If you believe something in your record is wrong or incomplete, you may ask in writing for it to be amended. The practice may deny the request in certain cases and will explain why in writing.
  • Accounting of disclosures. You may ask for a list of certain disclosures of your information made in the six years before your request, other than those for treatment, payment, operations, or made with your authorization.
  • Restrictions. You may ask the practice to limit how your information is used or shared. The practice is not required to agree, except that if you pay for a service in full out of pocket and ask that it not be disclosed to a health plan, the practice must agree. Because this is a private-pay practice, nothing is sent to a health plan unless you choose to submit a superbill yourself.
  • Confidential communications. You may ask to be contacted in a specific way or at a specific place, for example only by phone and not by email. Reasonable requests are honored.
  • Paper copy. You may ask for a paper copy of this notice at any time, even if you agreed to receive it electronically.
  • Breach notification. You will be told if a breach of your unsecured PHI occurs.
  • Choose a personal representative. Someone with legal authority to act for you can exercise your rights on your behalf.

Complaints

If you believe your privacy rights have been violated, you may complain to the practice using the contact details below, and you may complain to the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, Office for Civil Rights, at hhs.gov/ocr/complaints or by calling 1-800-368-1019. You will not be penalized or retaliated against for filing a complaint. You may also contact the Texas Behavioral Health Executive Council at bhec.texas.gov.

Changes to this notice

The practice may change this notice and make the new terms effective for all information it holds. The current notice is posted on this website and is available in the client portal and on paper on request. The effective date appears at the top.
