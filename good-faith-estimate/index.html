Your right to know the cost before you begin

Good Faith Estimate.

In short

  • Because the practice does not bill insurance, every client gets a written estimate of expected charges before the first session, and any time they ask.
  • Individual sessions are $150 to $175 for 50 minutes and couples sessions are $200 for 60 to 80 minutes. The free 15-minute consult is $0.
  • If you are ever billed $400 or more above your estimate, you can dispute the bill through a federal process. Keep a copy of your estimate.

What the Good Faith Estimate is

Under the federal No Surprises Act, health care providers must give clients who do not have insurance, or who choose not to use it, a written estimate of what their care is expected to cost. The estimate is called a Good Faith Estimate (GFE). Modern Balance Therapy is a private-pay practice, so this right applies to every client.

What the estimate includes

  • The expected charges for the services you are likely to receive, such as an initial assessment and follow-up therapy sessions, with the service codes used to describe them.
  • How many sessions are reasonably expected over the period covered, based on what you and Jackie discuss. Many estimates cover a 12-month period and are revised as your plan changes.
  • The provider's name, license and contact information, and a short explanation of your rights.

When you receive it

  • Before your first session, in writing, as part of your intake paperwork. If you schedule at least three business days ahead, the estimate arrives at least one business day before the session; if you schedule at least ten business days ahead, at least three business days before.
  • Any time you ask, whether or not you have scheduled, within three business days of the request.
  • When your plan changes in a way that would change the cost, such as moving from individual to couples work, you receive an updated estimate.

An estimate, not a contract

The Good Faith Estimate is not a bill and does not obligate you to receive any service. Actual charges can differ if your needs change, for example if you choose more or fewer sessions than first expected. You will always know the per-session fee before a session is held.

If your bill is $400 or more above the estimate

If you receive a bill that is at least $400 more than your Good Faith Estimate, you can dispute it through the federal patient-provider dispute resolution process. You must start the dispute within 120 calendar days of the date on the bill. An independent reviewer decides what you owe, and your care cannot be refused or disrupted because you used the process. The fee to start a dispute is modest and is refunded if you win.

Keep a copy

Save your estimate with your records. You may need it to compare against any bill you receive and to start a dispute.

Where to learn more

Visit cms.gov/nosurprises or call 1-800-985-3059 for questions about your rights under the No Surprises Act or to start a dispute.
